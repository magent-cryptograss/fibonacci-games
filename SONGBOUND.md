# SONGBOUND

A 16-bit style single-file RPG where **music replaces magic**. Working title —
rename it when the story exists.

Everything is in `songbound.html`. No images, no audio files, no libraries: all
art is drawn in code and all music is synthesised in WebAudio. Open the file in
a browser and it runs.

    file:///home/magent/games/songbound.html

or, served the way the other games here are:

    http://fibonacci0.hunter.cryptograss.live/games/songbound.html

## Controls

| | |
|---|---|
| arrows / WASD | move, navigate menus |
| Z / Space / Enter | confirm, talk, open chests |
| X / Escape | cancel, back |
| C / Shift | pause menu (status, songs, items, save) |
| mouse | works everywhere, and is the nice way to use the sprite editor |

In the sprite editor: `Z` paint, `X` erase, `Q`/`E` cycle colour, `F` fill,
`M` mirror, `U` undo, `R` clear, `T` cycle template, `Enter` done.

## The three systems you asked for

**1. Design your own sprite.** First play opens a choice: draw your own, or pick
one of eight ready-made. The editor is a 16×24 grid, 31 colours plus an eraser,
with horizontal mirroring on by default so you only draw half a face. A live
preview walks around in all four directions while you edit. The back view is
derived automatically — the face area gets filled with your hair colour — so
your character isn't staring at you while walking away.

The eight presets are generated by the same `buildSprite()` routine that seeds
the editor's templates, so drawn and picked sprites sit in the same world
without a style clash. Anything you pick can be opened in the editor and
changed (`C` from the gallery).

**2. Choose an instrument.** Eight of them, and the choice is not cosmetic. Each
one biases your stat curve *and* supplies the synth voice your songs are played
with — a banjo build genuinely sounds different from a dulcimer build, because
`VOICES.pluck` and `VOICES.bell` are different synths.

**3. Level-up choice.** Every level-up you pick **General** (ATK/DEF/MUSIC) or
one of the eight **elements** (fire, water, plant, ice, electric, earth, wind,
dark). Level 1 and every level ending in 5 or 0 is a **song level**: picking an
element there teaches you its next song. On any other level, picking an element
gives ATK and DEF plus affinity in that element. Max level 100.

Each element has a ladder of **8 songs**, 64 total. 21 song levels against 8
rungs means a specialist finishes a ladder around level 35 — after that,
picking that element again *upgrades* every song you own of it (`+1`, `+2`, …).

### One design note worth knowing

Only the General pick raises MUSIC. Taken literally that makes the pure
specialist — exactly the player the level system invites — end the game with a
permanently feeble stat behind every song they own. So **song power is MUSIC
plus three times your affinity in that element** (`songMusic()` in the source).

A generalist gets broad MUSIC that helps every element; a specialist gets deep
affinity that helps one. At level 40 they land at almost identical song power,
but the specialist owns the whole ladder and hits about twice as hard with it.
Your stated rules are unchanged — element picks still grant only ATK and DEF.

## Where to put the story

It's all data, deliberately kept away from the engine:

| what | where |
|---|---|
| Game title and tagline | `TITLE` / `SUBTITLE`, top of the file |
| Opening narration | `startGame()`, the `G.msg` lines |
| Townsfolk dialogue | `buildTown()` and `buildInteriors()`, the `npcs.push({lines: [...]})` calls |
| Boss encounter text | each map's `boss.intro` |
| Closing text | `END.lines` |
| Element names and flavour | `ELEMENTS` |
| Song names | `SONGS` |
| Monster names | `BESTIARY` |

Rename anything. The element *ids* (`fire`, `water`, …) are referenced by the
song tables and save files, so change `name:` freely but leave `id:` alone
unless you update both.

## The world

Town → overworld (meadow / wood / crag by latitude) → three cave floors → a
final room. Bosses are **The Gravebell**, **The Discordant** and **The Quiet**,
gated in order: each one's door refuses to open until the previous is beaten.
The overworld is generated from a fixed seed, so it's the same map every time,
with roads carved after the terrain to guarantee the landmarks stay reachable.

## Tuning

Balance lives in a handful of places:

- `DEF_K` and the three multipliers in `mitigate()` / `physDamage()` /
  `songDamage()` / `enemyDamage()` — global lethality. Defence gives
  diminishing returns rather than flat subtraction, because with flat
  subtraction the same monster is lethal at level 3 and harmless at level 15.
- `REGIONS[].tier` — how hard each area is. Enemy HP scales as `tier^1.6`,
  attack as `tier`, because player damage grows faster than player level.
- `xpToNext()` — the grind. Currently ~1M cumulative XP to reach 100.
- `applyLevelChoice()` — the per-level stat gains.

Verified numbers at the current settings: levels 1–50 run about 2–4 turns to
kill a regional monster and 6–11 turns to be killed by one. Bosses are 4–9
casts, surviving 6–10 hits.

## Known gaps

- **No story yet** — the text in place is placeholder, waiting on yours.
- **The world tops out around level 40–50.** The level cap is 100 because you
  asked for it, but there's no content tuned above roughly 50; those levels are
  for finishing song ladders. More regions would fix it.
- **Shop buys but doesn't sell.**
- **Single character, no party.** The design you described is one player — your
  sprite, your instrument, your level-ups — so party members would be a new
  system rather than an extension.

## Testing

The page exposes `window.__sb` for headless driving: `quickStart()`,
`goto(map,x,y)`, `forceBattle(region)`, `forceBoss(id)`, `win()`, `grant(xp)`,
and `sim()` / `simBoss()` which report damage numbers at any level and build
without playing. That's how the balance table above was produced.
